## Soft Skills & Professional Strengths

Strong technical skills only go so far — how you communicate, collaborate, and grow alongside your team is just as important. Here are some of the professional qualities I bring to every engagement.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Communication" icon="comments">
    I tailor my communication style to the audience — whether that's walking a non-technical stakeholder through a product decision or writing a detailed RFC for an engineering team. Clear, concise communication prevents misunderstandings and keeps projects moving.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Problem-Solving" icon="lightbulb">
    I approach problems systematically: reproduce the issue, isolate the cause, evaluate options, and ship a fix with a test to prevent regression. I enjoy the puzzle-like nature of debugging and treat every hard problem as a learning opportunity.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Technical Writing" icon="pen-to-square">
    Good documentation is a force multiplier for any team. I invest time in writing clear READMEs, API references, and architectural decision records (ADRs) so that future contributors — including future me — can move quickly with confidence.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Team Collaboration" icon="people-group">
    I thrive in cross-functional teams and believe the best solutions emerge from diverse perspectives. I actively participate in planning sessions, pair programming, and retrospectives to help the whole team level up together.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Code Review" icon="magnifying-glass-code">
    I give code reviews that are constructive and specific, focusing on correctness, readability, and long-term maintainability rather than style preferences. I welcome feedback on my own code with the same openness I try to model for others.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Mentorship" icon="chalkboard-user">
    I genuinely enjoy helping earlier-career engineers grow. Whether it's pair-programming through a tricky concept, reviewing someone's first pull request, or recommending resources, I find that teaching deepens my own understanding too.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
